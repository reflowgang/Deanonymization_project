What is irritating to the eyes are Chlorimates, chlorine bonded with nitrogen. Urine, dirt, leaves and grass are all sources. Chlorimates still kill germs, but the are irritating. You need to shock them out of the water by bumping the chlorine very high.  As long as the PH levels are good you can have a high level of chlorine without irritation. When the water is a bit cloudy and smells really "chloriney"  then you know that the chlorimates are high and will be irritating.  This concludes my Pool Operators license skills.
This is hardly breaking news guys. Learned all this in a Swimming Pool operations class in 1983.  You normally keep the pool at 1.5 PPM of chlorine. As the free chlorine bonds with any source of nitrogen (nitrogenous wastes)  it forms chlorimates.  Chlorimines/chlorimates are what you normally associate as smelling like chlorine at a pool, as the chlorimates start to build up the water is a bit cloudy and smells strongly of chlorine even though the total PPM is still around 1.5. This is when it fucks up your eyes.  To get rid of the chlorine you hyperclorinate, take the chlorine up to 4.5 - 6 PPM. This shocks the chloramines out of the pool.  The problem is that you need to keep the PH level on the ball when the chlorine is that high or it will be irritating. If you have the PH in the golden zone   ( 7.2 - 7.4 ) 4.5-6 PPM of chlorine is not at all irritating.   The reason pool operators don't shock the chlorimines out of the system all the time is because the ph has probably gone too high to correct. You can drop it down a bit with murretic acid but as calcium is leached out of the pool walls it will eventually set up a buffered solution, forcing the PH more basic and preventing the Ph from dropping. At this point you need to dump water and start fresh.  So all pool managers know about this, and dont give a fuck that the pool is irritating because chlorimates still kill germs.
