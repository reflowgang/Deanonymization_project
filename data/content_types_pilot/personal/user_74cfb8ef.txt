Ahhh, a typical "Just make more money" argument. For a lot of people, this simply isn't an option.  Let's say you work in retail or customer service and earn the minimum wage, which a lot of people do. The average full time contract in Britain is for 37.5 hours per week, and the national minimum wage in the UK is £7.20 (for a worker over 25 years old, whereas it gets even lower for anyone below that age.)  Hence, a 25+ worker can earn £270 per week, £14,040 per year, or £1,170 per month in pre-tax pay.  Now let's subtract taxation and National Insurance contributions from this figure. [Rather than bore you with lots of mathematical calculations, I found a website capable of calculating the income](https://www.incometaxcalculator.org.uk/?ingr=14040&amp;time=1&amp;yr=2017&amp;age=0#). This gives us a net annual wage (rounded to nearest penny) of £12,714.40 per year, £1059.53 per month, £244.51 per week, or £6.52 per hour.    Since UK employees are usually paid at the end of the month, we're now concerned with the net monthly figure of £1,059.53. [Let's subtract the average monthly energy bill on a small house/flat from that figure](https://www.ukpower.co.uk/home_energy/average-energy-bill), which is £61 per month, [then subtract food (£140) and internet (£20) from that monthly bill, on the assumption that the worker is living the austere lifestyle of a student.](http://www.ed.ac.uk/studying/international/finance/cost-of-living)  This means that we now have an income of £838.53 per month after taxation and basic necessities (gas/electricity, groceries/food, internet connection, which is almost mandatory in today's society, and is required to play World of Warcraft, so screw it, we're factoring this in as well.)  Since we're now concerned with the disposable income of a minimum wage 25+ UK worker, let's compare the monthly income of £838.53 per month to [average UK rent prices circa October 2015, according to statistics quoted by the Telegraph from Homelet:](http://www.telegraph.co.uk/finance/property/11984097/London-rents-now-a-record-108pc-higher-than-rest-of-UK.html)  * Greater London - There is no way that anybody on a salary that low can live in London with its average rent costs of £1,560 per month. London is just *that* heavily price gouged due to overpopulation, gentrification, and heavy demand for properties. Even most Londoners can barely afford to live in London on their increased average salaries compared to the rest of the UK.  * South East England - Perhaps if you're lucky enough to find a cheap property far away from London to rent, you could maybe live a very austere life in South-East England. Otherwise, good luck, because South East England is populated pretty highly with workers who commute into London on a regular basis.  * South West - At £872 per month, the average property in the South West is pretty unaffordable and will leave you living an incredibly austere lifestyle.  * East Anglia - At £809 per month, we finally have a point where we can calculate the percentage of disposable income that a WoW realm transfer priced at £17 would be: 838.53 - 809 = 29.53, and (17 / 29.53) *100 = ~57.57% of your disposable income.  * West Midlands - At £669 per month, the percentage of disposable income that a WoW realm transfer would cost would be: 669 - 838.53 = 169.53, and (17 / 169.53) * 100 = ~10.03%.  With the rest of monthly rent prices being very close to the West Midlands figure, and being at most ~£150 lower, let's not calculate the rest. Rent alone costs a huge amount of money for a Brit, and on top of having to pay out of our ass for other necessities, the price of a WoW realm transfer isn't justified just to be able to play a dying game with many servers resembling ghost towns.

I'm personally fine with the ability to pay for competitive advantages, so long as the business model is reasonable and doesn't require you to sink thousands of dollars a month in order to compete, just like with Clash of Clans. Then there's the question of what defines P2W in a video game.
