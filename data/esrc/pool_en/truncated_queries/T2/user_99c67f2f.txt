Ha, you don't have to be a hippie to disagree with the pipeline proposals. Well let's look at it this way: oil sands production in 2012 was 1.8 million barrels/day, and that oil got to market via existing transportation options, mostly existing pipelines. I can't find numbers for 2014, but in [2013](http://www.energy.alberta.ca/oilsands/791.asp) production was apparently 1.98 million barrels/day. Now the following pipelines have been proposed, to enable a major expansion of the oil sands: Keystone - 830,000 barrels/day Energy East - [1.1 million barrels/day](http://www.transcanada.com/news-releases-article.html?id=1746092) Transmountain - 600,000 barrels/day Northern Gateway - 525,000 barrels/day Add those up, and it's over 3 million barrels/day in *additional* capacity for moving bitumen from the oil sands, on top of the capacity already exists. Even if no pipelines from the oil sands currently existed, that would still be overkill at current production levels. [This Toronto Star article](http://www.thestar.com/business/economy/2014/01/31/why_is_keystone_so_important_to_supporters_and_opponents.html) lists most of the numbers I mention above, and also shows a helpful graph showing the Canadian Association Petroleum Producers' plans for oil production until 2030. And it just so happens that their plans include about 3 million barrel/day of oil sands growth, on top of all projects already operating or under construction. So the 3 million barrels of additional pipeline capacity will be necessary for the oil sands to expand production by an additional 3 million barrels/day. But at current production levels, they are basically not needed. Now don't get me wrong, if someone supports the rapid expansion of the oil sands, then it would be reasonable enough for them to also support these pipeline proposals. But personally, as someone who thinks we've all been kicking the can down the road on climate change for too long, and that we should significantly increase our efforts to begin transitioning away from fossil fuels, I don't support the further expansion of the oil sands (especially considering the magnitude of the proposed increase), and therefore think the pipelines would be a step in the wrong direction.
