Since you already know the basics, I think the best way to get started is to simply watch/listen to games. Preferably with a knowledgeable friend who doesn't mind answering questions. Or hang out in our Game Day Threads on /r/Cardinals! Just about everyone here is super friendly and approachable, no matter what the question may be. The nice thing about baseball is that it can be very stats driven (if you enjoy number crunching) or simple entertainment. I know practically nothing about the advanced stats, but I still thoroughly enjoy the games. #What To Watch For In... **Pitching** * There are usually 5 *starting pitchers*, who take turns pitching the majority of each game. Their specialty is pitching consistently well over a long time (100+ pitches). Our ace pitcher - Adam Wainwright - is out for the season, but you can see our May starting rotation on the sidebar. * *Relief pitchers* are brought in to replace the starter when he gets tired. They usually pitch one to two innings, and are generally a fresh arm without the stamina of a starting pitcher. * Finally, if your team is leading in the 9th inning, you bring in your *closer*. Ours is Trevor Rosenthal. The closer is your best reliever, and he's supposed to make sure the opponent doesn't make a final comeback. * There are also some specialty pitchers, but I want to keep this kind of simple. **Batting** - Due to some unfortunate injuries this season, our lineup has been pretty flexible so far. But I can still walk you through the basic strategy! * The leadoff (first) batter in a lineup is a guy with one of the highest On Base Percentages on the team. Between hits and walks, he somehow finds a way to get on base. * The second batter's job is to advance the leadoff runner while avoiding a double play. * The third batter traditionally has the highest batting average on the team, and is more powerful than the first two. * The fourth spot is the cleanup man. This should be your impressive power hitter: A guy who can hit a long ball and hopefully score at least one of those first three guys. * In the National League, the pitchers bat, generally in the 9th spot. The Cardinals are a National League team. In the American League, pitchers don't hit, and use a Designated Hitter instead. This is usually their cleanup batter, and he doesn't play defensively. His whole job is to hit long balls. I think that's a general walkthrough. Definitely watch some games, and listen to the announcers. They're a little dry, but they're full of interesting facts and they'll help you get to know our players better. We have a lot of personality on and off the field, and it's really fun to slowly get to know the team. I'm not the most knowledgeable fan in the world (or in /r/Cardinals, for that matter), but don't be afraid to ask questions. We both might learn something!

It's a confusing concept, but the designated hitter (DH) doesn't have to bat 9th. He usually bats 4th, as the cleanup batter, because his entire job is to be a batter. Cardinals fans don't have to worry about the DH much, since we don't play American League teams too often. &gt; What position makes the most impact defensively, besides the pitcher? Is it the first baseman? It's hard to say. Defense is baseball is all about 9 players working as cogs in one big machine. Individual performances are huge, but the player throwing the ball is only as good as the player catching it, and vice versa. There are always exceptions in baseball, but here's some generalities about a few positions: * Your "middle infielders" (second base and shortstop) are some of your most agile players. They have a lot of ground to keep an eye on, and not much time to cover it. * The center fielder should also ideally be very fast on his feet, and have a strong arm to get the ball back to the infield. * The first baseman actually tends to be a slower guy, since he spends a lot of time holding a base runner on first. In between pitches, a runner will try to get a head start and take some steps off the bag. A pitcher can throw to one of the bases and the baseman will try to tag out the runner before he can scramble back.
