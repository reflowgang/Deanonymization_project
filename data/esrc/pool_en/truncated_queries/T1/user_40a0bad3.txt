I don't know if it was a misread or a language issue, but the phrase "inconveniencing the way we do business here" does not automatically mean "inconvenience business". If a person wishes to eat breakfast at 6AM at a restaurant, then they will need to find a restaurant that is open. The person being inconvenienced if the business does not open until 7AM is the person who wants the breakfast, not the business itself (except through the transitive property).
