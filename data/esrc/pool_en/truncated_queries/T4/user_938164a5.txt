Yes, they do. &gt;Won't the pharmacy just give you the generic unless the doctor writes that the name brand is required? Generics don't exist for new medicines or reformulated old ones. &gt;Do doctors actually just do this? Yes. You've more than llikely been in a doctor's office when a person in business attire walks in and goes up to the counter. They're carrying a small rolling suitcase usually and a fancy tablet. The staff knows their name. They're a drug rep. Basically they exist to 'educate' doctors on the benefits and why they should prescribe thier repped medicines. They give out the branded office merchandise (pens, paper, etc) you probably have a few of lying around your house and often buy the staff and doctors lunch. Sometimes there are even sponsorships to conferences, that are nearly free vacations. Some doctors are truly unswayed, some listen for the free samples of the medicine they can give out to patients who are otherwise unable to afford said medicine, others don't care and will do anything for a free lunch. Often the medicine does work or there is no better alternative, so it makes sense to prescribe it. &gt;Do they only put the ads up in the time frame before a generic version is available? Not necessarily. There are people who genuinely only believe in name-brands. Also people who have no idea a generic exists. The doctor also has an option of telling the pharmacy not to dispense a generic. Sometimes the formulation of the generic can make people sick (or think it makes them sick) - though this also works the opposite way and people think it's the brand-name giving them side effects. There can also be multiple makers distributing generics and asking for the brand name will always give you a uniform pill type. You also still see ads for Motrin, Aleve, Tylenol, Claritan, and many others despite the fact you can walk into Wal-mart and buy twice as many generic pills for half-the price, there is value in a name.
