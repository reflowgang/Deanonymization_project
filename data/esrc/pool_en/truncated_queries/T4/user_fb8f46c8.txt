I quite like the style of gameplay that came from using a shield. Rolling is all about twitch reflexes, whereas using a shield is a much more strategic, methodical way of fighting. A lot of shield haters give the impression that you can just hold up a shield and block everything, but to have enough stamina to strike you need to bring your shield up only at the right time, and only just long enough to absorb the blow. One thing I learnt that I haven't heard many places, is when skating and you come across a crack or a bump or something that you think you might get caught up on, lift your front trucks over it and then just jump slightly with your back foot. Nowhere near the coordination needed for an ollie, and you will almost never get hung up even on big cracks and steps, because your weight is forward of the obstacle and you are pulling your board over it. You're far more likely to eat the floor if you try and hit it with your front wheels first, as it will act as a fulcrum point for all of your weight. It's possible to go up curbs like this, so if you aren't great at ollies yet you can still skate around the streets without having to constantly get off your board.

Here's the deal with almost every complete ever: The deck will be good. Enjoi and Almost are good brands, professional grade, no problem. This is where most of the quality is in a complete, as this is what people who are looking to buy will see first. The wheels will be fine as well. Wheels are easy to make, and there isn't much that can go wrong. [Unless you are really unlucky](http://www.reddit.com/r/NewSkaters/comments/39q1ru/one_of_my_bearings_became_extremely_loose_while/). The bearings and trucks will most likely be of mediocre quality, as these are the parts where it is easiest for the company to cut costs. This is just the way that completes work; it's the reason they are so cheap. When it all boils down though, the quality is a relative thing, and any name brand complete you get will do fine.
