Just to place this in context: the Indian Administrative Services exam is arguably one of, if not *the* toughest exam in the world. It covers an astonishing amount of material. The exam has two rounds. The first round has objective, multiple choice questions covering mathematics, general reasoning and general knowledge about Indian and global history, culture, science and arts. If you clear the first round (about 7,500 students out of around 900,000), you go to round II. Round II is *extremely* comprehensive. It has 7 subjective, essay-like papers covering everything from government policy to language, history, art, culture and science. The questions are obscure and demand a thorough knowledge and understanding of the subject. I know some really smart people who've studied two years straight and fail to crack this exam. And by two years straight, I mean *literally* leaving work and staying at home for two whole years just to study. With the assistance of a tutor. I consider myself smart enough, but if I was asked to appear for this exam, I'd run as fast as I could in the opposite direction.
You get to join the Indian Civil Services. This is an elite cadre of bureaucrats - a holdover from the Colonial days. Basically, these are people who run the country. The executive branch, if you will. The top 100 or so rank holders get to be join the Indian Administrative Services (IAS). Each IAS officer is like a Mayor. The next rank holders get to join the Indian Police Services (IPS). All of India's top police officers - police commissioners, state police chiefs, etc. Ambassadors and diplomats are chosen from this exam as well. Basically, these people have a lot of power and thus, respect in society. Imagine the kind of respect your mayor or local police chief gets, then multiply that by 10x since these are government bureaucrats and thus, hold office pretty much until retirement.
