Maybe a draft system should be implemented? It works well for many other fantasy sports.
So, a draft works by each team getting a turn to sign a driver who's within their budget. When it's a teams turn they get 60 seconds to announce their decision. A team then picks an available driver. On to the next team we go. Once the draft is over, teams are free to trade drivers between teams, or pick up drivers who were not signed during the draft. It would also introduce a new layer of economy, as teams could trade draft positions in return for coordinates / money. Most fantasy sports use something called reverse draft, meaning the lowest ranked team gets to pick first. That would mean that the driver market draft table for Model F1 2016b would look like this (Assuming Toro Rosso and Wolf are out): **First round:** * (New team) * BRM * Haas * Red Bull * Manor * McLaren * Lotus * Sauber * Williams * Ferrari * Mercedes **Second round** * Merceds * Ferrari * Williams * Sauber * Lotus * McLaren * Manor * Red Bull * Haas * BRM * (New team) As you can see, the reverse draft makes sure that a team that had a bad season, has a chance to improve their lineup, despite a low budget. A bit more reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draft_(sports) Edit: I realize that "New team" is probably gonna be Aston, but I swear that's not why I'm suggesting a draft. It works well as it guarantees a balance of power and gives everyone a chance at competing.

Yes, they are. The teams are only allowed 60 people in the paddock crew, so they all have multiple roles.
It's usually the mechanics who serve as pit crew. The engineers are very busy from the second the engine fires up to the second it stops. Every single byte of data has to be captured, analyzed and saved, whereas the mechanics deal with the gritty stuff, like changing parts and keeping the car running. Usually the individual mechanic is responsible for a specific part - for example the suspension - and will be working with the driver, engineers and strategists to come up with the best setup, based on the track, the condition of the parts and the preference of the driver.
