But even if they do, just accept it for what it is and be thankful for your luck. I disagree that people who "make a few lucky trades" should be taxed. TFSA was not setup to "cap" the tax free returns you can get in a TFSA. TFSA was setup to allow people to invest in many things including stocks -- some do well, others do poorly. If you take the 10,000,000 people who have a TFSA and they each opened a stock portfolio, there would be some outliers that made 10 to 100 times their money. An analogy is if you took all 10,000,000 people and had a coin flipping contest - for each round heads you stay in, tails you are out. The game would be expected to have about 100 people make 20 heads in a row! Here is a "lucky" investor: 2012: Invest $20,000 in West Jet. 70% return - $34,000. 2013: $34,000 + $5500 invest in CP Rail. 53% return - $60,000. 2014: $60,000 + $5500 invest in Amaya. 395% return - $238,000. So 5 trades (3 buy, 2 sells) over 3 years has turned $31000 into $238,000 for ~ 100% return CAGR... buying &gt; $1B market cap companies. Nowhere on the TFSA tax reporting form does it say you should pay taxes on this: http://www.cra-arc.gc.ca/E/pbg/tf/rc243/README.html
