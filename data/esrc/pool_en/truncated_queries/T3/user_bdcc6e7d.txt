Around the turn of the last century, students interested in going into physics were told not to bother because pretty much all that would ever be known about physics had already been discovered. We can look back now at just how arrogant and ignorant that thinking truly was. When Einstein wrote his paper on Special Relativity, it was an idea whose time had come. If he hadn't thought of it, someone else would have within 10 years or so. General Relativity, on the other hand, was such an extraordinary idea that without Einstein's discovery of it, we might still not know of it today. Considering how much technology upon which we depend wouldn't be here without it, imagine where we would otherwise be. Using words like "impossible", IMHO, are a bad idea when it comes to realms of knowledge for which we have only scratched the surface. They put ones mind in a box. I don't want us to make the mistake that our ancestors did of ignorantly assuming we have it all figured out. Getting a message from England to America involved sailing across the Atlantic for many weeks. If your goal was to get the message to millions of people, you could just forget it as it would be prohibitively expensive. Now imagine bringing such a person forward a few hundred years and showing them modern television or video conferencing via the internet from a cell phone with people all over the world. This would seem impossible to them and unbelievable if they weren't seeing it for themselves. As Arthur C. Clarke famously said, it would be "indistinguishable from magic." Is today's technology the end of the road? Is there no more to learn? Of course not. 200 years from now, we will have technology and capabilities that to us today living in 2016 would be indistinguishable from magic.
