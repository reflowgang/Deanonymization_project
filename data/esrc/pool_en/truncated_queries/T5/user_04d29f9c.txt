&gt; doubt bioplastic is going to pass for some uses like say medical IV bags and surgical tubing I work in medical device R&amp;D and you'll probably not like what I am going to say. Polycarbonate fittings, which are commonly used in surgery contain BPA. There is physically no other means of synthesizing polycarbonate except for polymerizing BPA. So, unless you ban polycarbonate and significantly reduce the surgical suite to use glass and metal fittings that can withstand high pressures, they'll not going away. Even if it is against European REACH regulations. Blood bags and surgical tubing is the opposite extreme. Both usually are made using plasticized (containing DEHP) PVC and there are other inferior alternatives (laminated LDPE, EVA, etc.) and more expensive alternatives (TPU) that cannot truly replicate the gas and water permeability of PVC. Turns out, the DEHP actually helps store the blood longer so the European Pharmacopoeia actually recommends the use of plasticized PVC for blood bags.
