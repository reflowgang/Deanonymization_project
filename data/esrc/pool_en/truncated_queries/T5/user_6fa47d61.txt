It wants more freedom to do its own thing as a member of the EU. This is where Cameron went wrong in his negotiations. He was negotiating to get a better deal from Britain without any other EU country getting anything in return. If he'd argued "We know you're sick of the UK getting special deals on EU membership and I want to make it so any EU country, no matter how small gets offered the same opt outs we or any other country negotiates". So when the UK opts out of the Euro, EU migration policies, Schengen, or anything else that any other countries get to decide whether they want to be part of the opt out. When Germany chooses to not accept migrants from newly joined EU countries, the smaller countries get to decide whether they wish to do the same. Currently only the heavyweight countries get to make opt outs, and that builds resentment among countries which lack the power or clout to negotiate their own deals. Cameron could have argued for a more flexible EU where even smaller countries get to choose how much they want to be a part of the project. Pretty much all members have parts of the EU they take issue with, but many are powerless to do anything about it. Cameron could have negotiated a change to all that, a change that would have benefited Britain and benefited the smaller countries which currently are forced to bend to the will of Brussels.
