Taxi's aren't the problem, it's the laws. If you're a taxi driver, you need to pay for a license to drive professionally. In order to keep this license, you need specific and clear markings on your car, insurance, etc. These are all mandated by the government, no real way to get around it. This situation is no different in the states (the cost is very high in NYC). Uber is providing a service that costs less because it skirts virtually *every* law that was put in place to protect consumers -- hence why it's cheaper than traditional taxi's. What Uber is doing is providing a transportation service that is better than traditional cabs. It would take very little work (ihmo) for cabs to adopt a model where it was 10x easier to get a taxi to come to pick you up, they simply haven't adopted with the times.

Your listed swing speeds of the driver are right in between R and S stiffness. Some shafts will easily have you in R and others will have you starting off in S stiffness. &gt; I'm happy with my distance which is 250 on a good drive At around 100mph, this is about the max drive distance you're going to get. I swing at around that speed and had a few 250 yard "bombs" yesterday. &gt; If I have to sacrifice 20 yards of distance for more control then I'll still come out ahead in the long run. Until you are absolutely dedicated to a workout routine to get your swing speeds much faster (we're talking 110/115+, which is crazy fast), the differences in the shaft are going to make nearly that much difference. The most common thing from a shaft that isn't stiff enough are the ball going really high into the air with both slices and hooks. You've indicated you're slicing the ball often, this indicates a path+face problem and not a shaft problem. &gt; Which from what I have found on other forums will be the result with a stiffer club. Is that information accurate? Golf is heavily about "feel". If you don't like the "feel" of something, it gets in your head and your confidence goes to shit, making you perform poorly. Having confidence in your equipment (aided by "feel") will make you perform better. Many golfers prefer the "feel" of a S club, but their performance could likely be better with an R instead (possibly with more weight in the shaft). What I'm saying to you is: at the swing speeds you're listing, the differences in those shafts won't make a dramatic difference. It's probably better than you fix your path+face issues, as that's the real cause of your slice.
