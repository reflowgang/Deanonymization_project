PSU = Power Supply Unit. It takes AC power from your wall and converts it to DC power for your components, since they can't use AC. I equate it to the heart of a computer. It's extremely important to get a good one. If you've ever connected a computer to a wall outlet, you probably connected the other end of the cable to something in the computer -- that's the PSU :) Likewise, if you've ever used a laptop, what most people might call the "charger" is a PSU.

People in the US can't just "take you in" and help you find a job. You're not authorized to stay in the long term: 3 months at a time at the most if you're from a [VWP country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_Waiver_Program#Eligible_countries), otherwise you need to apply for a [tourist visa](https://travel.state.gov/content/visas/en/visit/visitor.html) (which are valid from 1 to 10 years and allow you to visit up to 6 months at a time). You'd have to have in-demand professional qualifications in order to obtain a work visa. Of course, you could also get a student visa (F-1) and come study in the US, or an exchange visa (J-1) and come to the US for a limited time, for something like a cultural exchange, summer camp, stuff like that. The first and main requirement to applying for naturalization is to be a permanent resident for a number of years, a.k.a. a green card holder. I presume ports 22, 80 and 443 are open to 0.0.0.0/0. You can solve this easily simply by dropping port 22 from the security group, and re-add it with more specific source IP addresses than 0.0.0.0/0, assuming you know where connections will be coming from. If you want to set up a VPN, then yes, you'll need to set up something like OpenVPN to serve VPN clients. OpenVPN can live on the same server as your web app if you want, or you can set up a brand new server that will just run OpenVPN. Then you set your security group to accept connections on port 22 only from the subnet that OpenVPN places clients in, and then VPN users will be able to connect to your web server. So if the subnet you set up for OpenVPN's DHCP is e.g. 10.10.0.0/24, the security group assigned to your web server will look like this: - 0.0.0.0/0 (anywhere) -&gt; TCP 80 = allow - 0.0.0.0/0 (anywhere) -&gt; TCP 443 = allow - 10.10.0.0/24 -&gt; TCP 22 = allow https://docs.openvpn.net/how-to-tutorialsguides/virtual-platforms/amazon-ec2-appliance-ami-quick-start-guide/ has a guide on setting up an OpenVPN appliance in AWS.
