The KHL isn't the same as the NHL and should really be grouped in with the SHL and AHL in terms of quality. The NHL is the best league in the world, and ever since the fall of the old RSL and the merging of the WHA, no other league's accolades should really count. Again, this isn't a hatred of all things international. It's coming from an understanding that the KHL is majority populated by NHL rejects and fringe Russians who got homesick, so achievements in that league shouldn't have a similar impact.
&gt; ignoring accomplishments in the second best league in the world is stupid when talking about a hockey hall of fame This would only be true if the quality of the second best league is close to that of the best league. I'd like to illustrate a comparison for you. Look at the [2000/01 Florida Panthers](http://www.hockeydb.com/ihdb/stats/leagues/seasons/teams/0002342001.html). The NHL is Pavel Bure, the KHL is Viktor Kozlov, the SHL is Marcus Nilson, and the AHL is Mike Sillinger. Sure, Viktor Kozlov is the second best player on that team. But that doesn't really mean anything, because Pavel Bure is so much better than Kozlov that Bure is the only player on that team that really matters, when talking about 'best'. Not all leagues are equal, or even close, and not all accomplishments are equal, or even close. A championship shouldn't mean as much if it's against far inferior teams than the NHL can offer, and an MVP trophy shouldn't count for as much if the chief competition for the MVP trophy is a bunch of guys who couldn't manage to make the NHL. I agree that the Hockey Hall of fame should not be confined to just the NHL. But the fact of the matter is, RIGHT NOW, there isn't any other league in the world with a quality of competition that should necessitate consideration for Hall of Fame accolades. Of course, back in the 60s, 70s and 80s, there were other leagues that were able to match the NHL's quality. The WHA had guys like Bobby Hull, Mark Howe, Dave Keon, etc and a whole bunch more players who contributed to their Hall of Fame careers. I don't believe Mark Howe would be in the Hall without his WHA resume. And as you know, players from Czechoslovakia and Russia from the 60s through the 1980s are on par with the NHL all-time greats. The Czech leagues had guys who I believe should be in the Hall but aren't, such as Jan Suchy, Vlad Dzurilla, and Jiri Holecek. And the Soviet leagues had at least a dozen such players, some of which are in the Hall (Tretiak, Makarov, Fetisov, Larionov, Kharlamov, etc) and a bunch who are not, but should be (Sologubov, Vasiliev, Mikhailov, etc). Again, this is to say that I do not believe the Hockey Hall of Fame is an NHL-only league, but that it should only celebrate the best players, and since the Berlin Wall fell, I don't think any league in the world outside of the NHL has been of a high enough quality to contribute to a legitimate Hall of Fame resume.
