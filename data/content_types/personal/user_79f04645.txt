There are dangers and risks, especially for heavy chronic use...but I believe that you are overstating them and that it's no riskier than things that are currently legal.   &gt; 1a.) Marijuana is addictive, contrary to popular belief.   Yes, you are absolutely correct. THC can be addictive especially if used in adolescence. Biochemically addiction is addiction and there isn't much difference between addiction to heroin, alcohol, gambling and addiction to THC. However, it's important to point out that THC doesn't directly activate the addiction pathways like say Cocaine or Tobacco. Therefor when someone says weed isn't addictive, they can be both correct and wrong at the same time. **The more appropriate thing to say would be that weed isn't any more addictive than other legally available substances.** Unfortunately, that's a little too much nuance for a political statement so instead "weed isn't addictive" is what people go with.   &gt; 1b.) Lives have been destroyed by the previously stated addiction, without the involvement of legal issues.  Agree, addiction destroys lives. However, you are using humans as an example who are more complex than simply blaming one item on their problems. The individuals you mentioned may have underlining issues like depression that they are self medicating with weed. There isn't much difference between addiction to alcohol to curb depression than addiction to THC. I absolutely agree we should have a focus on mental health for *all* addictions. At least you are consistent that if we keep marijuana illegal we should also make alcohol/tobacco illegal, but I disagree that a general mental health arguments should be linked to a legal policy of prohibition.    &gt; 2.) Extremely slow cognitive function  Unfortunately we don't have a lot of studies on the long term effects of THC because the schedule 1 classification of THC makes it very difficult to actually study. The intelligence argument is kind of tough to make. A better argument to make your case would be that long term chronic use of THC has a negative effect on short term memory. So yes, anyone who says there isn't long term side effects to chronic abuse of marijuana isn't telling the truth. However, if we are going to make that statement then it's also important to ensure we are dealing with *chronic* use which doesn't necessarily relate to general recreational use.    However, once again from a legal policy standpoint it's hard to argue that these side effects are any worse than other legally allowed substances. Long term alcohol use leads to psoriasis and long term tobacco use leads to cancer. Yet both of those other substances are legally allowed to be abused. Once again, a more appropriate pro-legalization point would be the long term effects of marijuana are no worse than other legally allowed substances.    &gt; I think our discussion of whether we should or should not legalize marijuana (recreational usage) is being talked about incorrectly.
