Paul says that his readers should submit, because the superior-subordinate model of marriage was the norm among them.  If Paul's letter were addressed to all Christians at all times, then we could simply read his instructions and know that they were intended for each of us. However, in Ephesians 1:1, Paul addresses his letter to "the saints in Ephesus and in Christ," not all Christians for all time. So we cannot simply take the instructions at face value. Instead, we have to ask ourselves, "What made this a wise instruction to the Ephesians? And does the same make it a wise instruction for us?"  **What made this (the instruction for wives to submit) a wise instruction for the Ephesians?**  In Ephesians 5:23, Paul answers this question explicitly. "**Because** the husband is the head of the wife," which was true for most Ephesian households. In other words, when the husband is the head of the wife, **then** the wife should submit.  **Does this same reason make it a wise instruction for us?**  I would think the same is true today. **If** the household is arranged in such a way that the husband is the head, **then** the rest of the house should submit to that headship. However, in much of the modern world (especially in the West), that is not the household arrangement. In my house, for example, my wife and I share equal authority and mutual respect. We, therefore, do not meet Paul's precondition. So Paul's instruction does not apply to us.
