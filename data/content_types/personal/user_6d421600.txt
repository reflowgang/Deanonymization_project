The advantage is meant to be in the fact that the two layers use different vendors, so if a vulnerability occurs then it only affects one layer.  There is little to no point in having multiple layers of the same vendor, especially if it is a virtualized platform, as you lose the inherent advantage of the layers not having the same vulnerabilities.  There is also some advantage with the outer layer being a "plain" (i.e. not NG) firewall just doing layer 3 filtering, that way you can drop a fuck load of traffic before running it through your Next Gen firewall with all its inspection capabilities.  I've worked in environments where you have more than two layers between servers and the internet, and no device is allowed to jump more than one layer directly (most I've seen was client PC's being 5 layers in from the internet).  Things like Proxies in outer DMZ, load balancer in the inner DMZ and clients internal.  When it comes to things like VDOMS, I tend to treat them as a single horizontal layer,  there is no real inherent advantage of passing the same traffic back and forth through the same physical device.  Its more of a shared scalable resource for service providers rather than a way of implementing multiple layers of firewall.
