Many many years ago the broadcast affiliates would run syndicated shows when they weren't in network prime time. The morning and afternoon had soaps, some morning talk shows, and maybe a noon news, but the rest of it was syndication. One affiliate per market would get Wheel and Jeopardy, the other game shows were scattered about the grid, and then it was cartoons, sitcoms, and Westerns/dramas. This went through about 5-7pm when the affiliates would run their respective local news programs, the national news, and then prime time would start. That was followed by the late local news and the talk shows, if the network had one. Then it was back to syndication, which late at night devolved into infomercials and preachers. In the *really* old days some affiliates would shut off and run a test pattern.  Fox became the fourth network in the late 80s, and though its grid increased steadily over time, it didn't really reach the same solid programming status as the Big Three until it took over sports coverage in the mid-late 90s. So it was sort of a mix between a "real" network and an independent UHF-style station. It had X-Files and Simpsons and so on but there was no talk show (sorry Chevy) and, in my market, no local news. That gave it a bunch of extra time for evening syndication.  So there was a time in the mid-90s when the Minneapolis Fox affiliate aired three episodes of TNG every weeknight. Every. Night. Three hours of TNG. That's a season in less than two weeks. There was also a span in which it was two TNGs and a TOS, but it was still three hours of Star Trek *every night.* On *network TV.*  Now, to Millennials who grew up with on-demand binge-watching, three episodes a day probably doesn't sound all that insane. But DVDs, let alone streaming services, weren't even in our imaginations then. You could subscribe to a service where you got a 2-episode VHS every month or so, or you could tape the show when they aired the rerun. And that's what we did, all of us. Every Trekkie over a certain age has, or had, a pile of videocassettes in their entertainment center. So this was how we watched Star Trek. Indeed, this is how we *started* watching Star Trek. Every night. Saturated by it. It's no wonder we were hooked.  DS9 ran on either our NBC or UPN affiliate, I forget which. Maybe it switched during the run. When it went into reruns, I think it was stuck down at around midnight. Not sure if Voyager *ever* went into reruns here, and by the time Enterprise came along the idea of network affiliates filling up their off-hours with old shows seemed to have taken a backseat to judge shows, trashy talk shows, and reality, reality, reality.  But that's what I remember: TNG, always there on Fox 29. Watching TNG instead of doing homework, watching TNG instead of building social skills, watching TNG and enjoying every minute. I don't know if I would have been a Trekkie without those reruns. I certainly wouldn't have been so obsessed with Trek without them. I suppose having them all on Netflix is better for everyone.
