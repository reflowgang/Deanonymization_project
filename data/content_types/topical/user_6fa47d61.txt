So few crimes are committed with rifles (of any calibre) that it's not even a blip.  ---  We actually have no calibre restrictions here whatsoever (or restrictions on mag size limits). But you do need to show that you have access to land suitable for the calibre you wish to own.  For instance, if I wanted to own a 30mm recoilless rifle it'd be fine so long as I can show I have permission somewhere suitable to use it. There are a few MOD (military) ranges that allow civilians to use such calibres on their ranges, so as long as you're a member of a club that has access to such a range you're a'ok.  Also, the UK is far more permissive than most US states when it comes to silencers/suppressors (we call them moderators). Most UK shooting organizations and even most police forces recommend that they be attached to all rifles.    As far as we're concerned it's better for your hearing, less likely to spook your quarry, causes less noise pollution, and as far as the police are concerned it causes far less false call outs. They aren't seen as "tools of assassins" here, which seems to be the case in the US.  ---  We do have strict restrictions on action types though.... Other than .22 rimfire all rifles have to be manually cycled. Which basically means no semi auto rifles (semi-auto &amp; pump-action shotguns are fine though). You can also do the spin test.  Spin the egg on a worktop, stop it spinning very briefly with your finger then release.  The inertia of the white &amp; yolk still spinning inside the egg should cause the egg to start spinning again.  If it doesn't then the insides have started to congeal and solidify enough that the contents no longer spin independently of the shell.  You can also gauge 'how bad' an egg is by how fast it spins after being stopped. A freshly laid egg will spin just as fast as before you stopped it, one a few weeks old will lose much of its rotational speed after being stopped. A bad one won't carry on spinning at all.
