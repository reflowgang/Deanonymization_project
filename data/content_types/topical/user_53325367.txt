People in the US can't just "take you in" and help you find a job. You're not authorized to stay in the long term: 3 months at a time at the most if you're from a [VWP country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_Waiver_Program#Eligible_countries), otherwise you need to apply for a [tourist visa](https://travel.state.gov/content/visas/en/visit/visitor.html) (which are valid from 1 to 10 years and allow you to visit up to 6 months at a time). You're also not legally allowed to work in the US (just like I probably would not be allowed to work in your country without a work visa).  So you need a work visa. To get a work visa, you need to find a job in the US with an employer who is willing to sponsor you for a work visa. That employer will then file paperwork with the US immigration administration so you can then apply for a visa with your local US embassy. You'd have to have in-demand professional qualifications in order to obtain a work visa.  Of course, you could also get a student visa (F-1) and come study in the US, or an exchange visa (J-1) and come to the US for a limited time, for something like a cultural exchange, summer camp, stuff like that.  None of these things will ever allow you to become a US citizen, however. The first and main requirement to applying for naturalization is to be a permanent resident for a number of years, a.k.a. a green card holder. It's possible to become a permanent resident through employment, although, again, you'd have to be a qualified professional in a given field in order to have a chance. By far the easiest way to get a green card is to marry a US citizen. There's also the annual [green card lottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diversity_Immigrant_Visa).
