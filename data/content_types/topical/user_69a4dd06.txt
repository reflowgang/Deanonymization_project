The defendants will be able to pursue their attorney fees if granted.  Pao will be permitted to pursue an appeal but she does not have any basis to get extraordinary relief from the Court in the mean time.  So, for now, she can appeal the underlying trial.  But I doubt she has much basis to do so.   **How long will it take?**  It depends, but somewhere between 6 months and a year and a half to have it all resolved, depending on the Court of Appeal's back log and other factors.  My most recent California appeal took 6 months but that was on a procedural matter that was handled by written briefs only and no oral argument.    **Why do her lawyers keep going?**    1)  They want to get paid too.  They didn't take the case because they thought it was a joke.  Maybe they later found out it was a bad case as it developed, but having been put in that position during the course of discovery myself, you push ahead to see what you can do to salvage it because it is practically impossible to quit your client once you have filed.  2)  Appeals are an effective settlement weapon in some states.  Pao is probably doesn't have that much money.  An order to pay 250,000 in attorney fees is practically unenforceable.  So the Plaintiff's attorney says "We filed an appeal, how about you waive those fees or we can wrack up another 100k in attorney fees for you that you will also never see."  It's far off the 2.7 million she previously attempted to settle with but it protects her.  Now, it may be that she has funds or will have funds in the future but defendants may prefer the certainty of letting this issue go or simply end the cycle of press that this case is generating.  3)  It could be determined by the contract Pao signed with her attorney at the start of this ordeal.  Depending on the scope of the Power of Attorney agreement, the attorneys may have only signed up to take the case to trial or it could require Appellate representation.  Even if it didn't, bringing in another firm might create some later fee splitting issues if they ever figured out a way to earn a fee on this case.    4) Even if it didn't,it might be in their interest to handle the appeal to avoid malpractice claims.   Another firm might find errors in how they brought the case.   **Will they find out that Pao is bankrupt?**  Pao hasn't filed for bankruptcy.  So she is simply a poor target for enforcement.  The Defendants will get to pursue their fees.  Normal enforcement of a judgment is fairly straightforward. Usually, somebody cuts a check and some paperwork is filed with the court to show the judgment has been satisfied.  If they don't have the money, or claim not to, you can perform debtor's examinations, which is essentially an in court deposition about the location and value of all of her assets.  You can also file a writ of attachment.  Those are fun.  A party entitled to these fees can literally seize her bank accounts or other things of clear value to satisfy the order.  Even if Pao claims she has nothing, the party entitled to these funds can revisit this issue every several months to see if she has made money, acquired new assets, or hidden or transferred existing assets.  Given her status as the CEO of Reddit, there will be some money eventually.  If the parties don't settle during appeal, there is a good shot that they can see some money if they wish to pursue it.  Given the amount, I don't think I'd let it slide, 250,000 is a lot of money, but the enforcement mechanisms are cheap. It would costs next to nothing to continue to pursue this for years if the client so wanted.
