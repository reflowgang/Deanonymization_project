Two party preferred systems, like Australia, USA, and the UK (ugh theirs is such a *mess*) all have minor parties that never get votes because the major parties have the biggest media presence. If you think that people would put more effort into researching where to put their $30 than they would their vote, you're optimistic. Voter apathy is well understood, and is prevalent in this country too, despite our more equitable electoral system than the other nations I mentioned.  tl;dr: voters don't hear about minor parties, they don't vote for minor parties, they therefore wouldn't pick to support minor parties.  Actually, I found some numbers that might illustrate my point. So Australia has a bit over [~15 million voters](http://www.aec.gov.au/Enrolling_to_vote/Enrolment_stats/). In the 2013 federal election, Labor got 33.38%, Libs got 32.02%, Nationals got 4.29%, Greens got 8.65%, and all the rest got 21.66% (inflated because QLD LNP got 8%, which they normally don't, because people confused them with the Liberals). [Source here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_federal_election,_2013#House_of_Representatives) and [here](http://results.aec.gov.au/17496/Website/HouseStateFirstPrefsByParty-17496-NAT.htm). I realise that this is following a traditional media environment, but that's how your system would have to start, too.  So taking those numbers - 30 dollars per voter (D), 15M voters (V), and percentage of the vote (P), you can figure out how party funding might have looked after the last election.   Labor - $150,210,000 ($150M).   Liberals - $144,090,000 ($144M).   Greens - $38,925,000 ($39M).   Other - $97,470,000 ($97M), but this has to be divided between PUP, QLD LNP, Katter, all the independents, etc, etc. Suffice to say, very small numbers. A legitimately small party, like, say, the Australian Sex Party ([who got 0.61%](http://results.aec.gov.au/17496/Website/HouseStateFirstPrefsByParty-17496-NAT.htm), would have received $2.75M.    You're not allowed to donate to parties privately, right? Those funds have to run the parties for 3 years, not just pay for their advertising in the 2 weeks leading up to the election. If you guess that 75% of the funding goes to renting offices, overheads, and advertising, then 25% is staff funds. Sex Party gets $680,000. Divide by three for years. Down to ~$225,000. That could maybe pay for about four staff at $56,000/year. How many seats did they run for again?
All handled by local companies. There's an Australian branch for all of Microsoft, Sony and Nintendo. But generally, for most consumers, it doesn't matter - they buy from local retailers, and the retailers have to uphold the warranties themselves. It's based on who makes the sale to the consumer, not where the good originally comes from. Often this means that local retailers will have connections with manufacturers to effect repairs that go beyond retailers' capacities (though some, like EBGames, do have repair techs that can handle it in-house).  The most interesting precedent is, unfortunately, still afoot. [Valve is being not just investigated but actively sued by the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission](http://www.kotaku.com.au/2015/10/valves-court-showdown-with-the-accc-delayed-until-march-2016/). The issue is with Valve's refund policy not complying with Australia's requirements. I'm aware that Valve significantly revised that policy late in 2015, but it's still going to be fascinating to see how it pans out.
