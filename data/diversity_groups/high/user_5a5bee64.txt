Use a calculator if you don't believe me; both come out to the exact same number (~3.4 x 10^-6). This is basic elementary school math here. Do you not think that 9/27 is the same as 1/3? If you have a common number in the numerator and the denominator, you can divide both the numerator and the denominator by that number and get the exact same fraction. Another way of thinking about it: If every one of 292 million people bought a lottery ticket with a different number on it, then there would be a 100% chance of someone winning, with each person having a 1 in 292,000,000 chance of winning. If 292,000 people bought 1,000 lottery tickets each, with no duplicate numbers, that would be 292 million lottery tickets purchased - the same as in the above case - and again, there would be a 100% chance of someone winning. Ergo, the odds *must be* 1,000 times greater. Obviously in real life, people buy lots of duplicate lottery numbers, so even if 292,000,000 tickets are bought, you don't always get a winner, but if you're buying all the tickets, then you won't get any overlap because you know what numbers you picked.

For one thing, it is preemptive; for another, it is a matter of what is known as Assumption of Risk. Climbing is an intrinsically risky activity, and you are assuming responsibility for the risks you are taking; if you choose to engage in some risky activity, that is on your head, not theirs. On the other hand, negligence does not fall under the standard assumption of risk, and attempts at waiving such would generally fail. Indeed, you cannot waive any state law or public policy. Note, however, that they could, after their rock wall fell apart and hit you, offer you money in exchange for a waiver of further civil liability. If you signed THAT, then they would be immune to further suits (though if what they did was criminal in nature, they would still be criminally liable). Note that this sort of thing is what a settlement offer basically is - agreement to drop a suit in exchange for compensation.
