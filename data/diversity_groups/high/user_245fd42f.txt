Gas taxes are enacted in terms of cents per gallon, not percentage of price (so that revenue doesn't fluctuate wildly) Gas taxes are not adjusted for inflation. Gas taxes are for roads. NJ has a lot of old roads, but the 2nd lowest gas tax in the country. This is why the gas tax is going up. Raising the gas tax is an *outstandingly normal* thing to do.
Your skepticism is not based upon any facts. [Here's the revenue and outlays of NJ fuel taxes and transportation capital expenditures since 1985](http://www.state.nj.us/ttfa/financing/apprevenues.shtm). As you can see, the transportation capital fund has consistently received the entirety of the constitutionally-required share of the gas tax. More detail in the [state budget](http://www.nj.gov/treasury/omb/publications/16bib/BIB.pdf) (search for "motor fuels" tax).
Why? This means NJ can finally rebuild its roads! The gas tax is the main source of funding for state roads, and it hasn't been adjusted for inflation since 1985! It's currently the 2nd lowest gas tax in the country, despite the fact that NJ has much older roads than the rest of the country.
It's not about finding a single dollar (see [here](http://www.state.nj.us/ttfa/financing/apprevenues.shtm) for sources and appropriations of road funds in NJ), it's about correcting a broken formula where gas taxes are supposed to fund roads but can't because the rate hasn't been adjusted for inflation since 1985. Because gas tax revenues are so low, general funds need to be appropriated for roads, but this leads to putting off normal maintenance and highly unpredictable funding for major long-term capital projects. This deal reallocates funding to raise the same amount of revenue but do it in a predictable and sustainable way. Moreover, the tax is shifted to people that use roads more, so it's a more equitable sharing.

Since I don't live in NJ anymore, *I* haven't been paying for shit... but I would love to see the Pulaski finished (and as a train and tunnel user, I'd prefer if the Port Authority weren't paying for that). But the long and short of it is, the gas tax was too low to pay for all the needed road maintenance. So rather than increase the gas tax, the Transportation Trust Fund was created. The fund could issue bonds to fund large projects, and would be backed by revenue from the gas tax. But since the tax didn't increase, the fund eventually had to borrow more and more to do needed maintenance. Now the fund is broke (surprise!). Covering up the problem by creating a debt-issuing entity didn't work. You just need to pay for roads.
