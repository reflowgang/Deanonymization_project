*Kick***starter** It is the **starting point**, not the **end**. The actual funds to make a game are generally orders of magnitude higher than can be gotten by only kickstarter for a truly polished and fully developed big game. There are a couple of angles to this: --1) A studio without any other investments means their success translate directly into their profits and their IP are completely their own. This is a direct relationship between customers and developers which is good. --2) A studio that gets a publisher after the kickstarter and some time of development passed means they are in a better position to negotiate, with a publisher you expect to have more funds available and watch your back in case there is any mistakes and have a gigantic marketing engine at their disposal. It also increases the overall project development time which might not be good for the impatient but good overall for the quality and polish of the project. We have all seen the problems with the big kickstarter projects almost but not quite reaching their full potential(except Double Fine which was simply a botched job). Especially if you look at their expansion packs. You need time to test and refine mechanics as well as rewrite and edit text and redo levels. Wasteland 2's mixed results I believe about 50% of its quality was lost mainly because of development time. Things have gone cheaper but in the past a project of the same size would have had a magnitude more in development funds and it shows. --3) A studio that has a publisher already. At first this looks like its a marketing scheme and it *pretty much is* with publishers looking to gauge interest in a project and basically get what amounts some guaranteed pre-orders. **Nevertheless** it has some pretty **huge** implications. Publishers and investors are not a bad thing, **every other medium has publishers** and work well together with creators(except music) so its not a bad thing in itself. The problem resides in the **current form** of the game industry and its biggest faults. Budget and demographics. Right now publishers and their big AAA studios only target a specific although large demographic at the exclusion of everything else and implement absolutely astronomical budgets in its pursuit and maximum exploitation to recoup the cost. This is a fool's errant and they have to change. This type of Kickstarters are the first step in the evolution of publisher more towards a Hollywood model. The problem with publishers is the reason Kickstarter was born and was so successful so finally they got the memo and are starting to change. Although publishers using kickstarter seems kind of clumsy, and it is, it is a big step twoards the future. --4) **Now on to some not so nice implications**. The pool of Kickstarter money is limited and all this three instances are very much in competition. It is a zero sum game. Publishers have a possibility to drown the system in marketing and still fail in understanding demographics and budgets which can crash the system. Also while big projects would be advantaged from publishers/investors smaller scoped projects are about right on target for budget and development. FTL with its budget was pretty much spot on and realized its potential. So shrinking the amount of pie they have would be a loss. Since publishers do not care much about the money generated from kickstarter and only care about the interest it would be better to have a separate system and leave the money pie to those who need it. I think games having a in game questionnaire after they finish a game with statistics to gauge interest on different kind of projects would be much more efficient. Similar to how Shonnen Jump manga in japan does it that has monthly questioners in their magazines.
