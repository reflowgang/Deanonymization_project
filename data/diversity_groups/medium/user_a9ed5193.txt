Different people will like different gods due to stories or properties attributed to them in popular religious culture. What point does this serve? One must know what to ask of wish from the universe/god, and must pray in that direction - this is the basic tenet of any religion. If there exists a god who helps during financial calamities or helps improve them (Such as Lakshmi in Hinduism), the devotee focuses his/her thought on such a form/force and prays in that direction. In simple words, Idols, the attributes and stories, helps the devotee focus his thoughts and meditate more coherently. This basically helps with teaching how to meditate and devote oneself. Having idols will help the devotee feel he/she has served the almighty for their aspirations. Most idols are symbolism, and all practices aid in visual support for the recitation of the vedas/mantra (Sorta like religious hymns) in situations to aid the devotee. As one spends more time in devotion they may evolve to learn the forms of god are for focusing on worldly needs or desires, while the very same meditation learnt will help focus on a much higher goal, moksha (or unity with the bhraman/almighty). Sometimes it takes more than a few lifetimes to evolve from this phase. Idolatory is a basic expression of spirituality - prayer to gods (visual forms) or demi-gods is for accomplishment of worldly goals. Weather it works or not is a matter of another debate. If you go a little historically, when vedic religion spread, it did not as such destroy religious practices of the locals, rather incorporated them at best. Maya: Worldly activities --&gt; prayer with purposes (even world peace is a worldly purpose) --&gt; accomplishment of the same --&gt; prayer to gods attributed with qualities which will help one with the same --&gt;may include meditating upon the said god. Moksha: Medidation in breaking free from the maya --&gt; unity with the Bhraman. TL;DR: Idolatory helps in the spiritual evolution where the devotee realizes, not just intellectually, that purpose of meditation or faith or whatever is unity with the bhraman.
